A Little Book for Masha

A small beginning

This year,
it had to be a book.

Last year, a video. This year, something with pages—because that feels much more like you.

Not a biography. Not a dramatic speech. Just a collection of things I remember when I think of Masha.

Best opened with coffee.
Tea is also officially allowed.

Chapter one

The reader

Some people own books. You notice covers and bindings, love the smell of new pages, and casually plan a future home with at least 1,000 of them.

A completely reasonable minimum.

An important daily question

Coffee
or tea?

You wanted coffee. Someone mentioned tea. Suddenly the entire decision had to be reconsidered.

Coffee: for walks, mornings, bookshop plans and the photos that somehow fill your camera roll.

Tea: chamomile, music, one deep breath, and the firm belief that everything will be okay.

Final ruling: both.

The way your mind works

Curious

You do not just finish a story. You take it apart, connect it to the world, and send a page of thoughts because one idea would not leave you alone.

Observant

You notice the cover, the light through the trees, a voice in a song, the one detail everyone else missed.

Honest

If something matters, you say why. If you are unsure, you keep thinking. If the translation is ridiculous, you laugh first.

Creative

Paintings at midnight. Photos by the hundreds. Book discussions with actual chapter plans.

Russian · English · Spanish · and one accidental Portuguese lesson.

One timezone → another

A friendship with inconvenient geography

Your morning✦My evening

Thousands of kilometres. Different clocks. Messages that begin with “good morning” and end with “good night” for the other person.

Apparently, distance is excellent at complicating birthday timing and terrible at stopping a friendship.

12

The part I wanted to say clearly

You show up.

“Music. Chamomile tea. A deep breath. Everything will be okay.”

When exams piled up, you believed in me. When I needed to talk, you listened. You said “I’m here” so naturally that it never sounded like a performance.

I remember that. I appreciate your honesty, your patience, and the way you care seriously—without forgetting how to laugh five minutes later.

Thank you for being a very good friend.
